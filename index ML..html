Which pages decline next? Scoring content for refresh review

Capstone, Machine Learning track. Stanley Metone

Abstract

Content teams cannot review every page, so they need to know where to look first. We ask which pages are likely to lose clicks in the coming weeks. Using daily search performance for {{N_PAGES}} pages across {{N_CLIENTS}} pseudonymized clients, we build features from an earlier window and label declines in a later window. A gradient-boosted model reaches PR-AUC {{PR_MODEL}} against {{PR_BASE}} for a momentum baseline on held-out clients. The result is a ranked review list with reason codes, offered as decision support rather than proof of causation.

Introduction

The decision: with limited editor time, which pages should be reviewed for a refresh this cycle? A short ranked list with reasons is more useful than a full audit.

Data

Release v20260703 of the FlyRank pseudonymized warehouse: fact_content_daily_performance (latest-month sample, {{DATE_RANGE}}), plus dim_content and dim_clients for context. Pages with fewer than 30 clicks in the feature window were excluded because their rates are too noisy to score. No client names, domains, URLs or queries appear in this paper.

Methodology

Windows. The first two thirds of dates form the feature window; the final third is the label window. Label. A page is a decline if its daily clicks fell by at least 20%. Pages with no rows in the label window count as zero clicks. Features. Clicks, impressions, click-through rate, impression-weighted position, active days, and last-7 versus prior-7 click momentum. Baseline. Rank pages by falling momentum alone. Validation. One grouped split by client, so no client appears in both training and test. Leakage checks. Features use only feature-window dates, train and test clients are disjoint, and a model trained on shuffled labels scores near 0.5 AUC ({{SHUF_AUC}}).

Limitations

These are observed associations and directional signals. Nothing here proves how Google ranks pages or that a refresh causes recovery. The sample covers one month, so the label reflects a short horizon. Treating missing rows as zero clicks may overstate declines. Use the list to prioritise human review.
